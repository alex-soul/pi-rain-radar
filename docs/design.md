# Current design

## Implemented slice

An ordered loop of up to 13, 25 or 37 paired radar composites spanning a browser-selected two, four or six hours over a locally prepared regional basemap. Defaults are Coventry (52.40801, -1.51041), with a 1280 × 720 canvas at Web Mercator zoom 8 and overview zoom 5. The Map tab changes the centre, displayed name, both zoom levels and display time zone. This is a regional orientation map, not a street map.

The map fills the entire display behind a compact theme-matched translucent footer (76 pixels at the target size). A compact top-left stack contains clock, theme, Overview, MinuteCast and History controls, with radar health in the footer handle and current weather in the top dock; there is no heading or banner. At other aspect ratios, the map fills the viewport by cropping symmetrically rather than adding empty bands or stretching geography.

Node.js 24 LTS serves a dependency-light HTML/CSS/JavaScript frontend. Sharp validates, scales, crops, and composes radar tiles into two transparent PNGs per timestamp. The browser overlays those PNGs on the bundled SVG basemap. All display assets are local; there are no CDN fonts, map tile requests, or provider calls from the browser.

The backend checks RainViewer every five minutes, sorts/deduplicates `radar.past` into a bounded two-hour history, and retrieves only missing frames using viewport-dependent tiles, capped at source zoom 7, sequentially (12 tiles per timestamp at the supplied defaults). Requests are spaced by at least 850 ms (about 71/minute), including metadata. Zoom-7 radar is enlarged twofold for the zoom-8 display; this does not add source detail. Tile origins and the basemap use the same projection. Metadata host/path validation limits tile fetching to the expected RainViewer service.

A frame is published only after all requested tile images decode at the expected dimensions and composition completes. This verifies transport/composition, not that the provider has supplied every rain observation. The PNG and then manifest are renamed into place. Last-good state is persisted in a named Docker volume and loaded on restart; a failed fetch keeps that state. A hash of the viewport configuration is recorded in the manifest and image filename, preventing reuse of radar for a different centre, zoom, or canvas size and preventing browser cache collisions. The history manifest is replaced atomically with the available complete main/overview pairs. A failed timestamp is skipped so it cannot block later complete observations. Missing pairs still listed within the live window are retried on the existing five-minute schedule, including when no newer timestamp appears; completed views are reused. The window is bounded relative to the newest successfully completed observation, retaining last-good history if all newer frames fail. A historical gap uses the existing missing-slot indication; acquisition health remains in error when the newest eligible observation cannot be completed. Cleanup retains seven days of timestamped images plus a one-hour buffer. The current and previous sets plus the newest 26 images remain protected for handover/partial progress even during extended outages. Cleanup also runs after failed acquisition. Startup restores valid cached frames, requiring both views to decode successfully. Older main-only caches are upgraded by acquiring the missing overview images; they are not offered to the paired player until ready.

The frontend checks local status every 15 seconds and fully decodes both images for every timestamp in each incoming sequence before switching at a loop boundary. Images remain preloaded for playback without network calls. Frames advance every 650 ms, with a 1600 ms hold on the latest frame. Pause/resume and a keyboard-accessible timeline allow inspection; scrubbing pauses. While paused, incoming history waits until playback resumes. The 48 px, left-aligned and vertically centred footer clock follows the displayed observation, with its 20 px date alongside, using abbreviated weekdays and three-letter months such as Sun, 13 Sep (configured time zone). The date uses an 8 px clock gap to fit the existing observation area without moving the playback row at the target display size. The footer handle carries green/amber radar health and its expanded footer shows latest-data age (minutes below 60; decimal hours to one place from 60 minutes), using the newest frame known to the app even when playback is paused on an older sequence. Playback state and Past/Latest labels are omitted. The handle has an accessible label and tooltip. A failed refresh, lost local connection, or latest-frame age of 30 minutes turns the handle amber. Empty-cache acquisition shows progress. These are the 1× timings; browser-local speed scales both intervals through 0.5×, 0.75×, 1×, 1.5× and 2×.

## Data semantics and sources

RainViewer is the selected provider for ongoing personal appliance use, beyond the prototype. Its best-effort service has no SLA; local caching and visible freshness remain essential. A paid replacement is not planned unless observed limitations justify it.

- [RainViewer Weather Maps API](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/weather-maps-api.html): metadata timestamps identify composite frame generation, not the exact observation time of every underlying radar. The display labels them as frame times. Only past radar is used.
- [RainViewer transition constraints](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/transition-faq.html): plan around maximum zoom 7, Universal Blue, and 100 requests/IP/minute. Both views share the same request limiter. A completely cold 13-frame sequence requires 156 tile requests plus metadata, spread over roughly 2–3 minutes; each new timestamp needs 12 tiles.
- [RainViewer terms](https://www.rainviewer.com/api.html): personal-use API with best-effort availability and recommended local caching. The footer provides visible linked provider credits. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md for the separation between software licensing and provider data terms.
- [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/): public-domain geographic data permits bundling and offline use. A compressed geometry/name dataset in `assets/` is pinned to upstream revision `ca96624a56bd078437bca8184e78163e5039ad19`. It is loaded by a temporary backend worker during map preparation. Default generated assets remain versioned product assets.

## Current gaps

- Hardware deployment and interactive map changes are confirmed on the reference Pi; quantitative map-preparation performance remains unmeasured.
- RainViewer coverage/no-data masks are not yet shown. An uncoloured region must not be interpreted as a verified absence of rain.
- The default view uses zoom 8. Core UI and animation are accepted on the reference Pi; Natural Earth remains generalized cartography.
- Sequence publication, partial-failure recovery, and offline cache restoration have tests. Extended offline operation, power interruption, and Pi/ARM64 performance remain to be validated.
- Docker runtime is pinned to a Node 24 Bookworm multi-platform image digest supporting amd64 and arm64. Update the digest deliberately and rerun validation.
- The initial public release is MIT-licensed and marked as a pre-release. Published ARM64/AMD64 images are supplied from v0.1.2; normal Compose installs pull them. See [validation](validation.md) for confirmed on-device behaviour and remaining checks.

The main render size is fixed at 1280 × 720, the recommended landscape display resolution. Browser scaling supports other screen sizes; `xMidYMid slice` crops the map at different aspect ratios rather than stretching it. Responsive controls remain independent of render resolution.

Light/dark switching recolours the locally bundled basemap, labels, marker, health handles, controls, and translucent footer. Radar images and legend colours are unchanged. The timestamp uses the same 24 px outer padding as the right-side attribution at 1280 × 720. The footer remains 76 px high. Provider and map credits remain visible when the footer hides and retain working links, including during UI lock. External navigation uses a short kiosk warning.

The 46 px theme button displays a 28 px icon for its action: sun switches to light mode, moon switches to dark mode. Its accessible label also describes the switch action. The browser stores the selected theme in localStorage under radar-theme. A small local script applies the saved setting before the map paints; light is the first-use default. If storage is unavailable, switching still works for the page session. Preference is per browser/origin, not a server-wide or scheduled setting. Both basemap variants are generated by scripts/prepare-map.js and bundled for offline use.

The timeline shows 16 px start/end timestamps beside the slider, with no duration label. Play/pause, endpoint timestamps, slider and the 18 px frame counter share a single vertically centred row. The footer remains 76 px at the target display size.

### Scale, footer and widget layout

Interface → Display preferences are browser-local: scale shown and both dock auto-hide switches off by default. Auto-hide uses a single 15-second idle timeout, suspended during held pointer interactions and open dialogs. Tap or keyboard interaction reveals the footer. Scale follows its visible edge while retaining the main SVG scale factor. The settings cog follows its independent idle visibility. No acquisition or rendering changes. Maps remain north-up without a north arrow.

The SVG scale uses an explicit hidden attribute, since SVG does not reflect the HTML hidden property.

Overview and MinuteCast share a widget boundary derived from the auto-hide preference, loaded before restoring positions. Auto-hide allows the full viewport regardless of current footer visibility; permanent-footer mode reserves its height. Changing the preference triggers layout once. No repositioning follows the hide/show animation.

Widget stacking is session-local: pointer interaction, keyboard focus and opening a widget give it the foreground class. The scale and inactive widgets are below the active widget; header/footer controls stay above both. No DOM reparenting, position changes, polling or backend work is involved.

Refreshing starts with the footer visible and a fresh idle timeout; saved preferences and widget positions remain intact. Scale stays below widgets, and transient foreground order is not persisted.

Successfully completed images are reusable independently on retry. Fractional overview zoom uses rounded tile boundaries so adjacent raster tiles meet without gaps.

## Radar acquisition timing and recovery

New observations have a five-minute settling window measured from the start of the poll that first discovers them in the provider manifest. The scheduler and first-seen record share that timestamp, so manifest request latency does not accidentally add another five-minute cycle. The existing five-minute poll continues; no tiles are fetched for a waiting timestamp. First-seen times are bounded to the current manifest and persisted atomically in settling.json so normal restarts retain the wait. Missing/invalid state restarts the wait safely. On empty-cache startup only older observations may load immediately; the newest still waits. A lone newest observation leaves the initial loading state until eligible.

While waiting, retain the last complete sequence. When eligible frames are ready, merge still-relevant cached history, bounded to 13 frames/two hours ending at the latest successfully completed observation, and publish the main/overview pair atomically. Startup may temporarily supply 12 frames covering 110 minutes; the slider still spans two hours, with the absent slot marked. Poll timing can make the wait longer than exactly five minutes. Freshness continues to use the displayed cache's newest observation time. This reduces exposure to early provider revisions without adding tile requests, but cannot guarantee source completeness or detect later revisions to cached images. Existing cached observations are not repaired/refetched.

The expanded footer shows Next in N min only while a new observation is pending; it estimates the eligible scheduled backend check. Fetching… indicates active sequence acquisition. Existing local status polling carries this state without additional provider requests. Network/refresh failures suppress the estimate; freshness colour rules remain unchanged.

Latest and Next share a small muted font, separated by a middle dot, with no italics or approximation symbol.

Age uses compact wording such as Latest 16 min or Latest 1.5 hours, without an ago suffix.

On recovery when the provider's newest observation is at least 30 minutes ahead of the cached newest, older listed history bypasses settling, including any previously pending wait. Only the newest observation retains its first-seen delay. This also applies on restart with stale persisted history. Catch-up starts on the next successful scheduled manifest check; acquisition still takes time and last-good playback remains until the available complete pairs are published. Browser/OS wake does not currently guarantee an immediate backend check.

## Settings security

A bottom-right cog sits 8 px above the footer. Screen interaction reveals it independently of both docks; it disappears after 15 seconds idle, except while a dialog or held pointer is active. It opens settings directly on new installations. PIN protection is optional and off by default, with no setup prompts or reduced functionality. The PIN tab enables protection with a new six-digit value and confirmation, changes an enabled PIN after unlocking, or disables it. The PIN tab uses two aligned six-box rows with masked single-digit inputs, numeric keyboards, automatic advance to the next row/Save, paste filtering and Backspace/arrow correction. When enabled, access still uses the six-digit touch keypad. Existing salted PIN records remain enabled on upgrade.

- The PIN tab writes a random-salt scrypt hash to `/data/settings/pin.json` (file mode 0600, directory 0700). The persistent Docker volume is outside the repository/image. No plain PIN is retained. Disabling writes an atomic `{ "disabled": true }` record and removes the stored hash. Missing records mean protection is off; unreadable records fail closed. `node src/setup-pin.js` remains the compatible interactive host recovery path. No argument, --set, --reset and --enable all choose and enable a new PIN with hidden input; --disable atomically removes protection, and --status reports enabled/disabled without exposing the hash. Recovery requires host/Docker access, not the old PIN, and preserves other data. See the Quick Start recovery guide. Host administrators with volume access remain trusted.
- The backend checks the PIN and grants a random bearer token held only in browser module memory. Tokens expire after five minutes, are revoked on dialog close/page hide, and do not survive server restart. PIN changes invalidate existing sessions; browser enable/change closes settings, while disable keeps them open without a session. Unprotected settings have no five-minute expiry. An abruptly disconnected client leaves at most the remaining five-minute session lifetime.
- Failed attempts have a shared increasing delay (2 seconds up to 5 minutes), persisted across restarts. Only one PIN verification or configuration change runs at a time; competing first-time enrollments cannot overwrite the winning PIN. A successful unlock resets the delay; resetting the PIN does not bypass an active delay.
- `/api/settings` and its write routes allow access without a bearer token while PIN protection is off. When enabled, the backend requires a valid session even if someone manually reveals the HTML. Future settings read/write routes must use the same authorization check; POST `/api/settings/openweather` uses the same optional-PIN authorization and same-origin JSON checks, with bounded input. It stores/replaces or removes the key; GET settings returns PIN-enabled state, whether a key is configured, and current non-secret map configuration. POST `/api/settings/pin` uses the same origin checks, a 256-byte body limit and current authorization before enabling, changing or disabling protection. Stored PIN hashes and API keys are never returned. Without PIN protection, anyone who can reach the app can edit configuration or replace/remove the provider key.
- Mutations require JSON and reject cross-origin requests. Tokens/PINs are never placed in URLs or localStorage. Responses are no-store. There are no new dependencies or background polls; cryptographic work happens only at setup/unlock. UI expiry uses a one-shot timer while settings are open.
- Default Compose publishes port 3080 on host IPv4 interfaces for trusted home-LAN use; RADAR_BIND_ADDRESS=127.0.0.1 restores loopback-only access. Browser layout preferences remain local to each browser origin; server settings are shared. Plain HTTP does not encrypt settings entry; use an SSH tunnel or trusted HTTPS reverse proxy when the network is not trusted. The PIN does not encrypt network traffic or make this an Internet-facing service. Pi runtime performance remains to be measured.

## Stored radar history

History selects an end timestamp from stored paired frames for the current map. Live playback and History share the browser-local 2/4/6-hour preference, with 13/25/37 fixed ten-minute positions. Missing slots remain amber even behind playback progress; playback uses available frames and scrubbing selects the nearest available timestamp (earlier on ties). Keyboard navigation skips gaps. Changing duration in History preserves its end, pause state and existing return deadline. These gaps do not detect missing tiles within an otherwise valid provider image.

Images are retained for seven days plus a one-hour cleanup buffer. Available end choices retain the archive’s minimum two-hour boundary; longer windows can contain missing early slots. History accumulates only while this installation acquires observations; there is no historical backfill. Changing the map configuration prevents reuse of mismatched images.

An in-memory timestamp index is reconstructed once from paired PNG filenames at startup and updated as pairs finish. There is no database, additional tile download, re-rendering or background archive scan. Existing acquisition cleanup scans the retained filenames every five minutes. GET `/api/archive` returns available end timestamps; GET `/api/archive?end=<unix-seconds>&hours=2|4|6` returns one window, or 404 if unavailable. Both responses disable HTTP caching.

Choosing history replaces the browser's active sequence after both views decode. During the transition the previous and incoming window coexist temporarily, as with ordinary live handover. After adoption, the app retains only the selected decoded sequence; status polling continues for acquisition health without preloading live images. Browser-managed HTTP caching remains independent. Acquisition and live publication continue normally.

The compact picker places Date and Time side by side with a full-width Show action below, without explanatory text. Loading/errors remain visible when needed. After loading, the History toolbar control expands with the selected time range and stays expanded until returning live. It uses numeric dates without weekday or ordinal suffix and three-letter months, for example `12 Aug 09:50 - 11:50`; windows across midnight repeat both dates, for example `11 Aug 22:30 - 12 Aug 00:30`. Clicking the date range reopens the picker without collapsing it; clicking the History icon returns to live. Hiding the History dock removes both manual targets but does not stop automatic return. A fixed ten-minute deadline starts after a historical window loads; choosing another window restarts it, while pause/scrub do not. Timeout, page visibility and pageshow checks return to Now; refreshing also starts live. A failed live reload keeps the last images with the expanded History control retained until retry succeeds; the icon tooltip/accessibility label indicates the pending return. No per-viewer file leases are needed for casual viewing: the one-hour buffer exceeds the viewing deadline, and wake checks leave expired history. If a file becomes unavailable, selection fails without replacing the current map.

Storage depends on actual weather/compression. Measure image pairs on the target device; estimate seven days as average pair bytes × 144 × 7, plus buffer and filesystem overhead. Pi memory/SD performance and long-term operation still need hardware validation.

The expanded History dock has one two-pixel teal countdown outline around the entire pill, with no circular icon border. An SVG progress stroke replaces that edge clockwise from twelve o'clock over the ten-minute viewing period: the muted inactive control outline for the current theme. It is updated once per second by the existing clock tick, using the actual return deadline and clamping elapsed progress. A new selection restarts the ring; tapping the History icon returns immediately, while tapping its date range reopens the picker. No additional timer, continuous animation, provider request or backend processing is introduced.


## OpenWeather acquisition

OpenWeather health is shown by the weather drawer handle; radar health is shown by the persistent footer handle. Overview and MinuteCast have no separate LEDs. API settings shows the live connection result and last successful-fetch timestamp, with transient saving or actionable validation errors when needed. HTTP failures include the actual status code and a safe explanation; provider bodies and credentials remain private. Health uses existing status polling and the minute-level rendering guard, adding no network requests or timers.

`src/weather.js` uses only One Call 4.0: `/current` for current temperature/feels-like/wind and optional gusts, and `/timeline/1min` for next-hour precipitation. Both return `data` arrays; precipitation stays in mm/h. Each eligible refresh makes two bounded requests shared by all browsers. No pagination links are followed. It uses the radar centre coordinates, metric provider units and local wind conversion to mph; no hourly, daily or alert endpoint is requested. Requests are HTTPS with redirect rejection, 15-second timeout, bounded response bytes and validation of numeric fields/timestamps. Provider response text and request URLs are never logged or returned in errors.

The existing five-minute backend scheduler invokes a guarded refresh: at least ten minutes between attempts, no overlap, no requests while unconfigured. Explicitly saving a key requests an immediate check, including re-saving a key after activation. A persistent thirty-second setup cooldown bounds those manual checks; disabling/re-enabling or restarting does not bypass it. Saves during the cooldown return HTTP 429 with Retry-After and leave the stored key unchanged. Normal background polling retains its ten-minute interval. An off-cycle attempt may wait up to fifteen minutes for the following scheduled attempt. Last-good normalized data and the next-attempt timestamp persist atomically in `weather.json`, scoped by centre coordinates. Normal restarts respect the schedule. Each endpoint independently updates its last-good data and successful-fetch timestamp; failures retain that endpoint's previous data without renewing its age. `error`/`failures` concern current conditions; `forecastError`/`forecastFetchedAt` concern MinuteCast. A failure in one endpoint does not discard a successful response from the other. Retry errors are sanitized. Saving the key uses the settings authorization policy (PIN session only when protection is enabled); `settings/openweather.json` uses mode 0600 within the protected settings folder, outside image/source. This is restricted-file storage, not encryption against host administrators.

Weather metadata joins `/api/status`, so browsers add no polling endpoint/timer. The renderer short-circuits when cache identity, minute and availability/error are unchanged; radar frame playback does not redraw the chart. Temperature, feels-like and wind retain one failed poll, tinting only their numeric spans in the same theme-specific muted amber as retained gusts. Two consecutive failed polls or thirty minutes since the provider observation produces dashes. The failure counter saturates at two and persists with the sanitized error in weather.json so restarting cannot restore fresh styling; successful responses reset it, and location changes/key removal clear it. HTTP errors, network failures and rejected responses count once per attempted request, never per browser/status poll. Icons and units are not dimmed. Gust retention remains independent. Past forecast minutes are discarded from the visible chart as wall time advances; a fully expired/missing forecast shows unavailable. No data is converted into fabricated dry intervals. Provider attribution appears when configured. Current widgets stay current during radar history; no weather snapshots/archive or past forecast reconstruction are implemented.

Automated validation uses mocked provider responses and an isolated test UI. Each installation must confirm its own key entitlement, local forecast availability/quality and combined account usage. Initial Pi measurements and their limits are recorded in [validation](validation.md).

The successful OpenWeather connection message includes the last successful fetch timestamp in the configured zone (for example, 14 Sep 13:17).

Dashboard controls share theme-specific muted inactive outlines and teal selected outlines. The History countdown replaces its teal segment with the inactive outline colour. Weather-dock geometry responds to its content rather than a fixed width.

At the medium footer breakpoint (520 px and above within the compact/portrait layout), OpenWeather attribution occupies its own right-aligned line beneath Radar/Map attribution, with the rain legend above both. Wide layouts and the smallest stacked layouts keep inline attribution; the separator is hidden only when the weather credit moves to its own line.

The frame counter colours its total teal for a complete selected window (13, 25 or 37 slots), otherwise amber. It counts available paired images, not provider tile completeness. Missing positions remain amber on both played and unplayed slider regions.

The weather drawer grip displays OpenWeather health while expanded or collapsed: teal for usable data, amber for errors/missing/expired data or awaiting the first configured response, and white when unconfigured. It reuses the MinuteCast health calculation and existing refresh cycle; the drawer outline is unchanged.

Overview and MinuteCast omit title bars. Their focusable surfaces handle pointer dragging after a 4 px movement threshold, with native image dragging and text selection suppressed. Resize buttons are excluded from surface pointer starts and their keyboard events do not move the panel. Existing click-to-front, saved positions and saved dimensions remain; removing the overview header frees 32 px, while MinuteCast uses the extra space for its chart at saved sizes and has a shorter default/minimum height. No new polling or backend work.

Bright-theme dock text/icons use very dark grey, with quieter grey freshness text and matching playback controls. Light docks use a soft grey-green tint at roughly 68% opacity for readable charcoal text; dark dock appearance is retained. In both themes the footer background clips to its padding box so it does not double-paint the translucent handle join.

Light-mode expanded Clock and History controls share the docks' soft grey-green translucent background and charcoal text. Collapsed Clock and inactive History retain the standard light button background. In dark mode, expanded Clock and History use the dark dock background; their collapsed styles remain unchanged.

Light-mode MinuteCast shares the translucent grey-green dock background. Alpha applies only to the background colour; nonzero precipitation bars remain fully opaque.

Dark-mode MinuteCast also shares the dark dock background (#071410ad), applying transparency only to its background. Rain bars keep their solid fills.


The timeline has 13, 25 or 37 fixed ten-minute slots across two, four or six hours. Missing slots remain amber over playback progress in both themes. A cached slot model prepares the gradient; scrubbing selects the nearest available observation and keyboard navigation skips gaps.

Current weather includes wind gusts, in browser-selected units and order. The backend retains one last valid gust (mph and provider observation timestamp) in the existing weather.json cache, exposed as weather.gust. Missing/invalid samples and provider failures preserve it; repeated/older timestamps do not renew its age. Startup adopts a previous-format current gust at its original observation time. Coordinate changes and key removal clear it. Interface → Weather stores gustCacheMinutes under the browser-local radar-display preferences (60 default, whole numbers 1–1440), applied immediately on change. The renderer expires gusts by their own timestamp independently of other current readings, with a timestamp tooltip. Only the gust number receives a theme-specific muted amber tint when carried over rather than supplied by fresh current data, including provider-error fallback; the icon and units remain unchanged. Fresh readings and unavailable dashes use normal colours; absent/expired gusts are dashes and do not make otherwise valid weather unhealthy. A single retained record and the existing weather paint cadence require no archive, new endpoint or timer. The shared current-conditions response supplies all selected current readings; the forecast endpoint supplies MinuteCast on the same schedule.

## Installation and update boundary

Default Compose uses the published GHCR image, with latest or an explicit RADAR_VERSION. The development override builds local source. The named data volume persists independently of container replacement. HTTP status includes appVersion; HTML embeds its version so browsers can reload after a version change, waiting while Settings is open. Existing browser preferences remain origin-scoped. Runtime application changes need a version bump. See [upgrading](upgrading.md) for migration and compatibility limits.


## v0.3.0 acquisition, decoding and diagnostics

Radar settling is a shared, persisted setting in `settings/radar.json`, default on. Each acquisition snapshots it so a setting change cannot alter an in-flight operation. Disabling it removes the extra settling wait on subsequent acquisition without changing the five-minute poll or repairing cached tiles. UI preferences, including playback duration, never change provider request frequency.

The live status still exposes the recent acquisition sequence. Longer browser windows use the existing local archive, refreshed on relevant latest-frame/archive revision changes. Archive retention remains seven days. The browser decoder has one active and one replaceable queued request, two concurrent image decodes, bounded timeouts, cancellation of obsolete work, and reuse of current/pending images. There is no second permanent decoded history cache. Decoded pixels alone cost roughly 51 MiB for two hours or 145 MiB for six hours before browser overhead and temporary handover overlap.

Temperature is formatted to one decimal, speed/gusts to whole numbers. Temperature and wind units are independent. Optional humidity, dew point and direction come from current conditions without another endpoint. Eight direction-icon orientations and a sixteen-point compass abbreviation indicate wind origin.

UI lock is browser-local and independent of the shared PIN. It blocks dashboard interaction while keeping Settings, enabled auto-dock wake and all provider links available. PIN entry dismisses after 30 idle seconds. Provider credit stays visible at the bottom when the footer is tucked away, using its background/transparency. No permission to suppress RainViewer credit or disable its link is claimed; any provider exception remains future correspondence.

The diagnostic ring holds 25 fixed, safe events in memory; repeated events coalesce, and restart clears it. No raw provider response, URL containing a key or stack trace enters the UI log. Only the visible, authorized Log panel polls its endpoint (every ten seconds); capture itself does not depend on the panel being open. Status links are external navigation, not background health requests. The OpenWeather monitor is third-party.

MinuteCast distinguishes dry forecasts, partial future coverage and unavailable/error states. Empty data never becomes a claim of no rain. Messages include freshness or retry context; the existing paint cadence handles these changes without extra acquisition.
